National Geographic Endurance Review 2026

Named for Ernest Shackleton's ship, National Geographic Endurance shares Resolution's X-Bow hull and PC5 ice class, and carries the most detailed negative review of any single ship in Lindblad's fleet in this site's sample. For the fleet-wide picture, see the Lindblad Expeditions review.

This site found no direct contradiction to the 126-guest figure on Lindblad's own current page, despite third-party builder sources suggesting a post-delivery capacity change on some sister-ship configurations. 126 is used here as the verified figure.

History

Delivered in March 2020, National Geographic Endurance was built at the same CRIST (hull) and Ulstein Verft (outfitting) pairing as sister ship Resolution. She carries a Polar Class 5, Category A ice classification and the same X-Bow hull design.

Equipment

An ROV, undersea cameras and a hydrophone are standard aboard. A National Geographic photographer sails select Endurance departures, though not every one — the fleet's only guaranteed-every-departure ships are Explorer and Orion specifically.

FAQ

Is a National Geographic photographer guaranteed on every Endurance voyage?

No. Endurance carries a photographer on select departures; the only two ships in the fleet with a guarantee on every departure are Explorer and Orion.

What is National Geographic Endurance named after?

Ernest Shackleton's ship Endurance, lost in the Weddell Sea ice in 1915 and rediscovered on the seafloor in 2022.
