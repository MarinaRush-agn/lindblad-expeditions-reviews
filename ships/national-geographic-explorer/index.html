National Geographic Explorer Review 2026

One of only two ships in Lindblad's fleet with a National Geographic photographer guaranteed on every departure, National Geographic Explorer is the polar workhorse of the fleet — the smallest of the three Antarctic ships, and the most consistently praised for intimate scale. For the fleet-wide picture, see the Lindblad Expeditions review.

History

Explorer was built in 1982 at Ulstein Verft in Norway as Hurtigruten's MS Midnatsol, and passed through two more names and owners before Lindblad renamed her National Geographic Explorer in 2008. She is the oldest hull in Lindblad's current polar fleet, and carries a 1A Super ice classification.

Equipment

14 to 15 Zodiacs, 36 two-person kayaks, a glass-bottom boat, and an ROV rated to 1,000 feet, with a full-time doctor, wellness specialist, undersea specialist and photo instructor aboard. Lindblad guarantees a National Geographic photographer on every Explorer departure — one of only two ships in the fleet with that guarantee, alongside National Geographic Orion.

FAQ

Is a National Geographic photographer guaranteed on every Explorer voyage?

Yes. Explorer and Orion are the two ships in Lindblad's fleet with a guaranteed photographer on every departure; other ships carry one on select departures only.

Where does National Geographic Explorer sail?

Antarctica and the Arctic in their respective seasons, plus Norway, Greenland, Iceland, the Canadian Arctic, the Baltic and South America on other departures.
