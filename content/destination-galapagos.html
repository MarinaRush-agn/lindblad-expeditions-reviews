What reviewers say about Lindblad in the Galápagos

Based on this site's reading of the Cruise Critic sample for Lindblad's Galápagos programme

Naturalist expertise

This is the single strongest, most consistent praise pattern anywhere in Lindblad's review record — naturalist-guided wildlife encounters are described as the highlight of the trip almost without exception.

Cruise Critic
Small-group access

Both Endeavour II and Islander II run smaller passenger counts than Lindblad's polar ships, and reviewers credit this with closer, calmer wildlife viewing on daily landings.

Cruise Critic
Pre-departure administration

The same booking-office and pre-departure friction that recurs across Lindblad's whole review record also appears here, unrelated to the onboard or shore experience.

Why Galápagos reviews skew more positive than Antarctica's

This site did not find a confirmed operational reason for the gap and is not going to invent one. What the review text itself suggests: Galápagos wildlife encounters are closer and more reliably delivered than a single-species Arctic sighting, and the destination's year-round season and shorter itineraries may draw a different traveller mix than a five-figure polar expedition. Both are plausible; neither is confirmed.

FAQ

Which Lindblad ships sail to the Galápagos?

National Geographic Endeavour II and National Geographic Islander II, both smaller than Lindblad's polar fleet, running year-round departures.

Is the Galápagos programme good for families?

Yes — National Geographic Explorers-in-Training, Lindblad's under-18 activity programme, runs on most Galápagos departures, with a minimum age of six on most family sailings.
