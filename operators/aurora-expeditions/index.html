Aurora Expeditions Reviews 2026: The X-Bow Fleet's Score, Explained

Aurora Expeditions ranks third in this ten-operator comparison with a total score of 79.8, built on a short landing rotation, the second-highest traveller-rating range in the ranking, and a distinctive hull design credited with a smoother Southern Ocean crossing. It suits travellers who want a short rotation without Poseidon's single-ship limitation; it does not suit travellers who have been put off by the communication complaints noted below.

The fleet

Aurora operates three Polar Class 6 vessels: Greg Mortimer and Sylvia Earle, both 132 passengers, and Douglas Mawson at 154. All three use the distinctive X-Bow hull — an inverted bow shape that cuts through waves rather than slamming over them, which reviewers consistently credit with a smoother Drake Passage crossing than conventional hulls. All three exceed the 100-guest shore limit, so a short rotation applies fleet-wide.

What reviewers say about Aurora Expeditions

Aggregate rating: 4.5–4.6 (Cruise Critic / TripAdvisor)

Expedition team

Geologists, historians and ornithologists on staff are the most consistently praised element on both review platforms, credited with maximising time ashore whenever weather allows.

Cruise Critic / TripAdvisor
Ship & cabins

The X-Bow hull is repeatedly credited with a smoother ride; a minority of reviewers note limited storage for wet expedition gear.

Cruise Critic / TripAdvisor
Communication

A recurring complaint concerns pre-departure and in-voyage communication — itinerary or activity details not matching what was sold — and slow refund handling when a voyage was disrupted.

Cruise Critic / TripAdvisor
Landings

Itineraries are described as packed with Zodiac cruises and shore landings, with the crew pushing to get guests ashore even in difficult weather; a few reviewers found logistics rushed at busier sites.

FAQ

What makes the Aurora Expeditions X-Bow hull different?

The inverted bow shape cuts through waves instead of slamming over them, which reviewers on all three ships credit with a noticeably smoother Drake Passage crossing than conventional expedition-ship hulls.

Are Aurora Expeditions Svalbard cruise reviews different from Antarctica reviews?

The same fleet and expedition-team praise recur in both regions; this page and the ranking focus on the Antarctic sailings specifically, where the 100-passenger-plus capacity drives the short-rotation landing style.

Does Aurora Expeditions have helicopters?

No. Unlike Quark's Ultramarine, no ship in Aurora's fleet carries a helicopter; its expedition-depth score of 78 reflects the X-Bow hull and activity roster rather than aircraft-supported landings.
